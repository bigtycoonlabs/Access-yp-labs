What she does today

All of this runs now. Several businesses, several states, one place. She works from your record rather than a checklist, and she tells you where every answer came from.

Filings, licences and renewals
Fifty states and DC. Every rule carries the source it came from and the date it was checked, and where the fee is not confirmed she says it is not confirmed rather than guessing. Ohio does not require an LLC annual report — most tools tell you it does.
Documents, including the ones that are not there
Storing files is the easy half. She works out what should be on file from what your business actually is — a contractor means a W-9, a partner means an operating agreement — and an expired certificate leads, because nobody believes they are covered by one they never collected.
Customers, as promises and money
What you owe them and what they owe you, kept apart, ordered by what is late. Not a CRM. There is nothing to keep up to date — people appear when something is outstanding and leave when it is settled.
Your team, and exactly what each person can see
A bookkeeper sees the money and not the filings. Adding a contractor raises a W-9; adding your first employee raises workers comp before their first day, which is one of the genuinely dangerous moments in a small business that almost nothing warns you about.
And she tells you before it is late
An expensive filing is raised at thirty days, fourteen, seven and the day before. By email, so it reaches you when you are not looking at this.

What she will not do

  • She will not invent a filing, a fee or a deadline. Where the rules are confirmed she says the number; where they are not, she says that instead of guessing.
  • She has confirmed figures for twelve states so far. For the rest she has the deadline and tells you the fee is unchecked.
  • She does not do your books. That is Arbo, in YP Flow, and anything about money goes to him rather than being half-answered here.
  • She will not tell you nothing is due when she could not check. A failed look is not a clear day, and she says which it was.
  • She is built for your business, not for everything. Ask her about your dinner and she will point you somewhere better.
