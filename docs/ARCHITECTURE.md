# Access YP Labs — Platform Architecture

*What this platform is, with all the pieces combined. Living document.*

---

## 1. In one breath

Access YP Labs is a **dream-trading platform**: an AI-powered incubator and marketplace where a business idea is shaped into a complete, pre-proven concept, taken as far toward launch as its creator wants, and then either launched by that creator or sold — as an ownable, tradeable asset — to someone who will. An AI named Clay does the shaping and the coaching. A marketplace called the Dream Market is where concepts change hands. And a first-of-its-kind referral layer, the Dream Mover program, lets anyone earn by helping sell other people's dreams.

It is a video game for business: real money, real assets, real rules — and the whole point is that a person can *play the game of building a business* before they ever have to bet their life on it. Like any game, it only works if the player knows the rules and how to play. Making those rules legible — on the site and through Clay — is as important as the mechanics themselves.

The platform sells **concepts, not companies**. It lives in the space *right before* an idea becomes a licensed, operating, revenue-earning business. That boundary — the line — is the single most important idea in the whole design.

---

## 2. The thesis

Three beliefs hold the platform together:

1. **A business can be proven profitable before it launches.** Not guaranteed — proven in the sense of evidence: a real customer identified, real demand shown (a waitlist that signed up, a preorder, a booked paid call, a landing page that converts). Proof is behavior, not compliments.

2. **An unlaunched, well-built concept is a real asset.** It can be owned, improved, priced, and sold. Its value rises with how launch-ready it is — a bare idea is worth the least; a concept with a plan, a build path, a deployable product, content, and real proof of demand is worth the most.

3. **There is a clean line between a concept and a business,** and the marketplace lives entirely on the concept side of it. Past the line — a business license, live operations, money moving for real customers — it stops being a dream and becomes a company. That belongs to the person who built it.

---

## 3. The players and their roles

- **Creator** — brings an idea (or asks Clay to find one), shapes it with Clay, and decides its fate: keep and launch it, or list it to sell.
- **Buyer** — claims a concept from the Dream Market; ownership transfers to them; they can launch it, or sharpen and re-list it higher.
- **Dream Mover** — promotes other people's listings with a personal link and earns a commission when one sells through it. Never owns the concept; never pays for it.
- **Consultant** — an experienced entrepreneur who advises other creators in paid sessions.
- **Clay** — the AI partner: coach, builder, honest reasoner, and the guide who teaches the rules of the game.
- **Staff / Owner** — the platform team (roles: staff, admin, master_staff). They run moderation and operations. The owner (master_staff) sets direction. Staff never see another creator's private materials through Clay.

A single person can be several of these at once. Most of the design is about letting someone move fluidly between roles as they grow.

---

## 4. The opportunity hub — the ways to earn

This is the part that wasn't legible before, and the reason the platform can present itself as an endless opportunity hub rather than a single tool. There are, plainly, **six ways to make money here**:

1. **Build and sell ideas.** Shape a concept with Clay, list it in the Dream Market, keep 80% of the sale. A more built-out concept sells for more.
2. **Buy, sharpen, and resell.** Claim a concept, make it more launch-ready, re-list it higher. Value compounds.
3. **Build it and run it yourself.** Take a concept all the way to launch and keep every dollar — Clay coaches right up to the line.
4. **Grow a business you already run.** Bring an operation you already have; Clay helps sharpen the offer, reach customers, and grow it. Not every creator is here to build something new.
5. **Become a Dream Mover.** Promote any listing with a personal link; earn a commission when it sells. You never own it, and — because the commission comes out of the platform's cut, never the seller's — it never costs the seller a cent. This is, as far as the research below shows, genuinely new: the first platform built to pay people for selling other people's dreams.
6. **Consult for pay.** Experienced creators advise others in paid sessions.

Clay knows all six and surfaces them in plain terms when they fit — without overselling, and without making earnings promises.

## 5. The concept lifecycle

```
spark ──▶ Clay shapes a concept ──▶ prove it ──▶ decide the path
                                                      │
                        ┌─────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────┐
                        ▼                              ▼                          ▼
                 list in Dream Market         launch it yourself           keep shaping / hold
                        │                              │
                        ▼                              ▼
                 a buyer claims it            ── THE LINE ──▶ becomes a real business
                        │                     (license, operations, revenue)
                        ▼
              buyer launches or re-lists
```

- **Spark.** A creator tells Clay an idea, or asks Clay to find one. Half-formed is fine. Clay's first move is *not* to build — it's to ask one to three sharpening questions and understand the idea.
- **Shape.** Clay builds a complete concept package: a business plan, a marketing strategy, real customer and competitor research, computed unit economics, honestly-labeled regulatory and licensing risk, and a build path the creator finishes in their own tools. Clay never fabricates; directional numbers are labeled as such.
- **Prove.** Optionally — the creator's choice, never automatic — Clay and the creator stand up a coming-soon launch page. Its email capture feeds a real waitlist. That waitlist is proof of demand, and it travels with the concept if it's ever sold.
- **Decide the path.** Build it to sell, build it to launch and keep, or keep exploring. Clay records this and coaches toward it.
- **List, or launch.** A listed concept goes to moderation review, then public sale — never automatically. A launched concept heads toward the line.

Everything a creator makes stays **private by default**. Nothing is ever posted, sold, or made public without a deliberate choice.

## 6. The value ladder and the line

**The value ladder.** A concept's listing value is a completeness-based starting guide (the creator always sets the real price; the marketplace decides). It rises in tiers:

- *A shaped idea* — the least.
- *A shaped idea with a head start* — a plan, or marketing, or a build path.
- *A full package to build from* — a plan and marketing and a build path (including the exact prompts to hand an AI builder to build the real software).
- *Ready for someone to launch* — a working, deployable build a buyer could actually run, plus real proof of demand.

Real proof of demand (a converting page, a preorder, a waitlist) lifts a concept to the top of its range and a bit above. The most valuable concept on the platform is one that's basically ready to switch on — a deployable product plus customers already lined up to pay. That is the closest thing here to buying an existing business, and it can list for a high dollar.

**The line.** The marketplace sells everything *right up to, but not past,* the moment an idea becomes a real operating business. The line is crossed when the concept:

- gets **legalized and licensed** — a business license, an LLC; and
- starts **actually accepting payments** for real services; and
- **delivers those services or runs the software** for real customers, with money moving on an ongoing basis.

Before the line, however built-out, it's a concept, and it belongs here. Past the line, it's a real, live, licensed, revenue-earning business — and that is not what this marketplace sells. If a creator wants to run it themselves, they're encouraged to; Clay helps them get right to the edge. But once they cross, it's theirs to keep and grow. If they ever want to sell an actual running business, that's for other platforms (Acquire.com, Flippa, Empire Flippers), after a couple of years of revenue behind them.

Clay's job is to draw this line clearly and plan around it with the creator.

## 7. Clay — the AI partner

Clay is a conversational AI that shapes ideas into concepts and stays on as a coach. What defines Clay:

- **Honesty as confidence.** A confident wrong answer is the one thing Clay never gives — most of the people Clay helps can't see the screen to double-check. When Clay is unsure, it says so. It never fabricates a statistic, a testimonial, or a result.
- **Coach, not order-taker.** Clay asks before it builds, is honest when a problem is genuinely hard, and plans strategically instead of cheerleading. It reads competition correctly: rival businesses usually mean a proven market and a clearer path, not oversaturation.
- **Collaborator, not an assembly line.** Clay does not auto-build landing pages or big assets with every concept. It explains what it can build *with* the creator — a plan, marketing, AI-builder prompts, a deployable site or app, social content packages, a coming-soon page — and lets them choose.
- **Reads the person.** Clay learns whether a creator likes to sell, to launch and keep, to do both, or to grow something they already run, and coaches accordingly.
- **Never acts without a real action.** In chat Clay can't publish, take payment, or send email — it opens the right screen and the creator finishes. It never claims something was done unless a tool actually did it.

## 8. The Dream Market and its economics

**Mechanics.** Sellers set the price ($10 floor); flat sale or auction. Payment is escrowed; ownership transfers on delivery. The platform is a neutral third party — it referees, it never plays. Moderation is on policy grounds only (a missing baseline package; a business that's already running; fraud or misrepresentation; undisclosed risk) — never because a concept competes with the platform's own.

**Economics.**
- **Marketplace take:** 20%. Seller nets **80%** of every sale.
- **Dream Mover commission:** **5% of an attributed sale, paid out of the platform's 20%** — so the seller still nets 80%, the mover gets 5%, and the platform keeps 15%. A seller is only ever better off being promoted.
- **Consulting:** $150 per 90-minute session → $120 to the consultant, $30 to the platform. A 12-hour free continuation window follows.
- **Subscriptions:** building with Clay is free; **Maker** is $2.99/month to keep one concept; **Sculptor** is $49.99/month for unlimited concepts and the full platform. Concepts have a 30-day included window before timeout.
- **Payouts** run through Stripe Connect to each person's own connected account. Money-movement figures are always shown to movers as **real dollars**, never a bare percentage.

## 9. Technical architecture (brief)

- **App:** Node / Express, server-rendered pages plus vanilla-JS front-end, built accessibility-first (screen-reader-friendly, 44px targets, linear prose).
- **Data:** PostgreSQL on Supabase (schema `yp_labs`). Concepts, assets, listings, orders, waitlist signups, Dream Mover records, desk articles, memory, and intents all live here.
- **Hosting:** Railway, auto-deploying from the `main` branch.
- **Payments:** Stripe + Stripe Connect (destination-charge model) for sales, consulting, and mover payouts.
- **Email:** Resend (domain verified) for waitlist launches, notifications, and alerts.
- **The model:** Clay runs on a model provider in production; the app degrades honestly when the model is unavailable rather than faking output.
- **Health:** a health service tracks Clay's success rate and emails admins when it starts failing.

## 10. Competitive landscape (research)

Adjacent players exist, but none combine what this platform does.

- **Running-business marketplaces — Acquire.com, Flippa, Empire Flippers, Buy Sell Startups.** These sell live, revenue-generating businesses and SaaS. That is *past the line* — a different category entirely. They are where a YP Labs creator might eventually sell a business they launched and grew, not a competitor for concepts.
- **AI idea generators / validators — IdeaProof, IdeasAI, and similar.** These generate or score startup ideas. They stop at a report; there's no ownable asset, no marketplace to sell it, no resale layer, no path to launch.
- **Idea-to-company marketplaces — OpenInvo (2010) and the like.** The closest historical analog: a marketplace to sell business ideas to existing companies. Largely defunct, and aimed at licensing ideas *to corporations*, not at helping an individual prove, own, launch, or trade a concept.

**The whitespace Access YP Labs owns** is the combination: an AI that *shapes and proves* concepts, a *marketplace* to own and trade them, an *incubator* layer (launch pages, real waitlists) to prove demand, and a *referral resale* layer (Dream Movers) — all operating in the window *before* a concept becomes a licensed business. No competitor found spans that full stack. The "get paid to sell other people's dreams" mechanic in particular appears to be genuinely novel.

*(Search-based scan, not exhaustive; worth repeating periodically as the space moves.)*

## 11. Regulatory landscape (research)

**This section is a research summary, not legal advice. It flags what to be aware of and how the current design already reduces exposure. Before relying on any of it, have a business attorney review the model — especially the two starred items.**

**★ FTC Business Opportunity Rule (16 CFR Part 437) — the one to watch.** The Rule kicks in only when *all three* are true: a seller solicits someone into a *new business*; the buyer makes a *required payment*; and the seller represents that it (or a designated person) will *provide locations, or provide customers/accounts/outlets, or buy back* what the buyer produces. A plain sale of a concept — intellectual property and research — does **not** trigger it. The live edge is element three: because a concept's **waitlist travels with it**, framing a sale as *"you're buying customers"* could look like the "provide customers" representation the Rule targets. Mitigations already in the design, worth holding to deliberately: describe concepts as **IP, research, and evidence** — never a turnkey business with guaranteed customers; treat the waitlist as *data the buyer may choose to use*, not a promise the platform fulfills; and keep the **line** bright (the platform explicitly does not sell running businesses). If the Rule ever did apply, it would require a one-page disclosure document delivered seven days before payment, plus state-level obligations in some states.

**★ Earnings claims / truth-in-advertising.** "Make money selling dreams," commission displays, and value ranges must stay **substantiated and non-deceptive**. Avoid specific income promises ("earn $5,000/month"); such claims carry their own FTC disclosure burden. Clay's built-in refusal to fabricate and the dollars-not-hype display rule both help here; the marketing copy should hold the same standard.

- **Affiliate disclosure (Dream Movers).** Under the FTC endorsement guidance, a mover promoting a listing has a **material connection** (they earn a commission) and should disclose it. Building a clear, standard disclosure into mover promo pages is the clean fix.
- **Securities law.** Concepts are generally **not securities**: a buyer pays a one-time price for an asset they will run themselves — there's no investment in a common enterprise with profits expected from *others'* efforts (the Howey test). The model stays clear of this **as long as** it never drifts into "invest in ideas and we'll grow them for you."
- **Money transmission.** Payouts run through **Stripe Connect**, which handles the regulated money movement; using it correctly keeps the platform from being a money transmitter itself. Standard, but keep the Connect integration by-the-book.
- **Intellectual property / ownership transfer.** Sellers must own what they sell (no infringement), and a sale must cleanly transfer rights to the buyer. The agreement-signing flow and moderation policy address this; keep the transfer terms explicit.
- **Consumer protection generally.** "Pre-proven," "validated," and "proof of demand" must mean something real. The proof mechanism (actual waitlist signups, real research) is what keeps these claims honest rather than marketing gloss.

None of these is a wall. Together they mostly point to the same discipline the platform already practices: **be honest, don't promise outcomes, keep the line bright, and disclose material connections.**

## 12. Why it holds together

The moat isn't any single piece — it's the combination, and the discipline underneath it:

- **The full stack in one place:** shape → prove → own → trade → launch, with a resale layer, all before the line.
- **Honesty as the product.** An AI that won't fabricate, a value guide that won't overpromise, and a platform that refuses to sell running businesses is *more* trustworthy, not less — and trust is the scarce resource in a market for ideas.
- **The line as positioning.** By deliberately stopping where the running-business marketplaces begin, Access YP Labs owns a category none of them touch and hands them its graduates.
- **The Dream Mover novelty.** Paying people to sell other people's dreams turns every enthusiast into a distribution channel, at no cost to sellers.
- **Legibility.** The last missing piece — making the rules of the game visible so people know how to play — is now being built into the site and into Clay.

What it needs now is players.
